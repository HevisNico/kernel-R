## What is established

- **The verdict.** XOR3_27735 (K03) and XOR3_88529 (K01) are refuted at K0 under the frozen rule: one differing letter refutes.
  - 27735: on key 1/0/0, the seal predicts `ssFF` and the network gave `sssFF`.
  - 88529: on key 1/1/0, the seal predicts `ssFF` and the network gave `sssFF`.
  - In each case one inserted `s` accounts for both differences the comparator lists, at positions 2 and 4.
- **Everything else matches the seal.** In both instances the other words, the four outputs (the XOR truth table), the cell bits and the skew slots agree with the seal.
- **The refutation of 27735 is reproduced on a second computer.** The duplicate on K12 gives the same comparison (`equals_original: true`), and check_duplicate finds the traces identical (`identical: true`).
- **The two counted XOR instances that held** are 46230 at K0, and 75255 at K0 and at K3 through its three noisy rounds.

## Conclusion

In both XOR instances refuted at K0:
- the inputs, simultaneous in stage D, arrive in the network 1.6 µs apart and later in the slot (mean arrival T+12.0 µs instead of T+2.4 µs);
- of the two first-layer cells, the one with the smaller model stop margin emits one pulse later than predicted;
- the four final outputs still conform to XOR.

## What is not known

- **The analog margin.** The differential analog margin at the comparators is not recorded, in the network or in stage D. Neither the port comparators (`vs`, `vrefsn`) nor the cell's common comparator (`vq`, `vrefmn`) are saved.
- **What the numbers above measure.** 376 / 100 000 is a margin in the model. The peak of v(p) measures the latch output.
- **What caused the late emission.** Whether the spacing between the ports, the common delay, or both caused it is open. "The spacing sufficed" is reserved for the result of the replay below.
- **The sample.** Two cases cannot set a margin threshold.
- **A smaller margin that held.** A second-layer cell of 88529, c2_0 (group 0, an `either` cell), has a smaller K0 model margin (316) and held. In all four of its rounds its network record shows an arrival on port 1 at T+30.8 µs and none on port 2 (`out_K01/XOR3_88529/N0/noisy_record.json`; margins in `out_K01/XOR3_88529/D/fit_d.json`). It shows that a small model margin can coexist with a correct prediction. Being a one-port read, it does not test the hypothesis of a spacing between two inputs.

## Replay planned

This replay is a reconstruction made after the fact, and it will be reported as such.

For each refuted cell and for its witness cell, the isolated stage-D deck is replayed with the same history and parameters. "Same history" means that the preset and the first two lesson pulses are kept exactly as they were, and only the times of the two inputs of the third read change:
1. the reference, 2.4 / 2.4 µs, which must reproduce `F`;
2. a late but simultaneous pair, 12.0 / 12.0 µs;
3. the exact network arrival times, in the cell's own port order.

The port comparators, the common comparator and p are saved, together with the pulse times.

How to read the result:
- If case 2 gives `F` and case 3 gives `s`, the spacing between the ports suffices in this replay.
- If case 2 already gives `s`, the common delay comes first.
- Cases 2 and 3 have the same mean arrival time, so their contrast tests the effect of the inputs' separation alone.
- If both give `F` while the network gave `s`, that is informative too: reproducing the arrival times alone on the isolated deck did not reproduce the refutation.

Any timing margin drawn from this replay is a new rule. It would need its own prospective test on new seeds.
