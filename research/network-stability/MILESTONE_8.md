# Milestone 8 — schedule confluence

[ScheduleConfluence.lean](ScheduleConfluence.lean) projects the guarded state to its pending probes and write counters. Two configurations agree when their queues are permutations and their counters are equal. The chronological log is an observable of a particular order. Each zero-error step selects a pending occurrence and applies the existing guarded update. The milestone 7 selection relation projects into this model, and a certified noisy step projects to the same clean step when its one-sided read margin holds.

## Local diamond

For two probes pending at distinct cells, each guard uses its own cell's write count and the bias and drive stored in that probe. Processing the other probe leaves the guard unchanged. The two orders make identical decisions and produce identical counters. Pending descendants are the same multiset. Exact queue equality can fail, as the checked example with two self-routed FIREs shows. The chronological logs can also differ. If an error depends on the event index, swapping events can change which error a probe receives. The diamond applies to clean decisions and to noisy decisions that agree with them by their margins.

At the same cell, the checked diamond allows either of the following hypotheses at every reachable configuration.

- **A. Cell exclusivity.** At most one pending occurrence targets each cell. The model stores a single counter per cell, so exclusivity of individual ports would require a model with independent port memories or a further separation proof. An initially exclusive queue can lose exclusivity when a FIRE branches.
- **B. Interleaving margin.** For distinct pending probe types at the same cell, the clean score $s$ must have room for the writes that can precede the probe and its one-sided read error. If at most $k$ further writes can occur, a nominal silence requires $s<0$ and $s+dk+h<0$. A nominal FIRE requires $s\ge0$ and $s+\ell\ge0$, where $[\ell,h]$ is the error interval. This directional rule uses the fact that writes raise the score. The local diamond needs $k=1$ at the common starting state and uses the clean specialization. A proposed certificate measured from a different baseline must justify that the intervening writes are additional to that baseline. Two selections of the same probe type already agree modulo queue permutation.

The milestone 7 counterexample violates both conditions at the common state after the trigger. Two probes target cell 1, and the warm probe has score $-1$ with dose $2$, so even at zero error it loses its silence margin after one preceding write. The milestone 6 ring satisfies A at every reachable state for every positive size. It starts with one token and emits at most one successor. The perpetual ring has the same property after its one-time training probe is consumed.

## Global conclusions

With milestone 5's strict scalar certificate, every scheduler step decreases the permutation-invariant queue potential. The Lean proof of Newman's lemma uses induction on that rank, with transport along queue permutations. Every reachable state can drain and every pair of exhausted runs gives identical write counts and stored memory. The equivalence preserves memories, and allows different logs. For noisy runs, the read margins must cover the selected probes along their clean orders. The certified-trace theorem composes the per-step margin implications with the order theorem and gives equality of terminal memory.

For perpetual traffic, there may be no empty-queue normal form and the ranked Newman proof is unavailable. The proved local diamond is strong enough to join any two **finite prefixes** by finite continuations without a rank. As a consequence, if two prefixes each have memory frozen under **every** possible continuation, their write vectors agree. Milestone 7 gives eventual stabilization along a run. That alone does not establish a schedule-independent limit, since a continuation that reaches the join can differ from the actual infinite order. A claim about all infinite schedules would need an additional argument about progress or fairness. In the one-token traffic phase of the perpetual ring, the next probe is forced.
